# Proxy alignment: which fit-time number actually predicts KL

_Generated by [`code/proxy_alignment.py`](../../code/proxy_alignment.py): CPU-only mining of results and cache metadata that already existed. No fit, no model load, no GPU. Regenerate with `python code/proxy_alignment.py`._

**Why.** The W2 step lowered the activation-weighted L2 objective by 1.7% and KL got 11% worse. That is a Goodhart signature, and it makes every future "better objective" proposal a gamble until we know what the proxies we already have do and do not predict. This document is that measurement, over every row the project produced.

**Answer in one paragraph.** Inside a fixed fit mechanism both proxies rank behavioural damage perfectly (Spearman +1.000 in every family measured). Across fit mechanisms at identical bits they part company. Plain `rel_err` has no resolution left: the whole W1 effect fits inside a 0.52% move of it, an elasticity of 273 against ~24 in its own family, and against the matched-seed-budget control (KL 1.121121) it moves the *wrong way*, 15.6% worse for a 2.57x KL improvement. The activation-weighted proxy predicted W1 *on trend* (elasticity 3.2, inside the 1.6-6.7 range of its own budget sweeps). One mechanism change later it stopped: W2 lowered the same weighted objective on all four rows and raised KL on three, and the whole `c12p4wr` family sits 7-13% above the `c12p4w` family's proxy-to-KL curve at equal weighted error. So W2 is **not** an outlier: the curve is mechanism-dependent, which means the weighted objective is no longer a function of damage near its own optimum. The cheapest next measurement is therefore not a better objective; it is finding out whether the objective's weights are simply under-estimated from 12 calibration prompts (that probe has since been run, see the README's finding 4).

**The two proxies fail this split in completely different ways, and the rank statistic cannot tell them apart.** Both land on the same mean within-group rho, both for the same reason: each gets the big call right (the unweighted fit is worst) and each inverts the `c12p4w` vs `c12p4wr` pair in three groups of four. A blind proxy and a Goodharted proxy score the same on rank agreement; only the spread column distinguishes them.

**Reading.** Plain `rel_err` at the W1 step has an elasticity of 273, against 23.5 inside its own family. Take that family elasticity as the proxy's honest exchange rate and it predicts that a -0.52% move in plain rel_err buys KL 1.818 → 1.607. The measured value is 0.436. The proxy is not merely imprecise here; it is off by a factor of 3.7 on a quantity it is supposed to rank, because it was not measuring the thing that moved. Against the one-variable control it is worse than imprecise: plain rel_err moved from 0.2192 to 0.2534 (15.6% *worse*) while KL improved 2.57x.

The weighted proxy, by contrast, moved by a large and *ordinary* amount: -36.0%, at an elasticity of 3.2, squarely inside the 1.6-6.7 range its own within-family budget sweeps show. **W1 did not surprise the weighted proxy at all. It moved along the proxy's existing curve.** That is the entire empirical case for the weighted objective, it is a good one, and §3 is about the point where it stops.

## 3. The W2 row: an outlier, or does the trend itself bend?

W2 (exact coefficient search) is a strictly tighter minimisation of the *same* objective W1 introduced: its candidate set contains the nearest-rounding point, so it cannot increase the objective on any block, and the harness selftests assert exactly that. It lowered the objective on every row.

| outlier budget | wrel `c12p4w`→`c12p4wr` | Δ objective | KL `c12p4w`→`c12p4wr` | Δ KL | elasticity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed_only | 0.17035 → 0.16737 | -1.75% | 0.545002 → 0.544878 | **-0.02%** | 0.0 |
| awq_p0.5 | 0.16530 → 0.16251 | -1.69% | 0.485797 → 0.489034 | **+0.67%** | -0.4 |
| awq_p1.0 | 0.16161 → 0.15894 | -1.65% | 0.436118 → 0.485679 | **+11.36%** | -6.5 |
| awq_p2.0 | 0.15509 → 0.15261 | -1.60% | 0.422626 → 0.468470 | **+10.85%** | -6.4 |

Objective lower in 4/4 pairs; KL higher in 3/4. As a sign test that is p = 0.625 two-sided: suggestive, not decisive, on four paired rows. The interesting structure is not the sign count, it is the geometry.

**Is `c12p4wr` an outlier against the weighted-proxy → KL trend, or is the trend itself mechanism-dependent?** Take the `c12p4w` family as the reference curve (four rows, monotone in both axes), interpolate it piecewise-linearly, and ask where each `c12p4wr` row lands on it at *its own* weighted error:

| `c12p4wr` row | its wrel | KL the `c12p4w` curve predicts | KL measured | residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `awq_p2.0` | 0.15261 | outside the curve's range | 0.468470 | — |
| `awq_p1.0` | 0.15894 | 0.430593 | 0.485679 | **+0.055086** (+12.8%) |
| `awq_p0.5` | 0.16251 | 0.448276 | 0.489034 | **+0.040758** (+9.1%) |
| `seed_only` | 0.16737 | 0.510034 | 0.544878 | **+0.034844** (+6.8%) |

All 3 in-range rows sit **above** the reference curve, by +0.0348 to +0.0551 KL (mean +0.0436, i.e. +8.8% of their own KL). A single outlier would be one row off a curve; this is an entire family displaced in one direction. Inside itself the `c12p4wr` family is perfectly well behaved (Spearman(wrel, KL) = 1.000); it simply lives on a different curve.

The fourth row needs no interpolation to make the same point. `c12p4wr_awq_p2.0` has a **lower weighted error than every `c12p4w` row in existence** (0.15261 against a best of 0.15509) and lands at a KL that 2 of the 4 `c12p4w` rows beat (0.468470). If the objective ranked damage, that row would be the best row in the table.

Pooling the two weighted families gives Spearman(wrel, KL) = 0.857 (exact p = 0.011, n = 8) against +1.000 inside each family separately. The degradation is the family offset, not noise.

**The contrast with W1 is the whole finding.** W1 and W2 are both changes to the fit mechanism at byte-identical bits, and both lowered the weighted objective. W1 moved *along* the weighted proxy's own curve (elasticity 3.2, inside the 1.6-6.7 range of the within-family budget sweeps). W2 moved *off* it (elasticity -6.5 on the same row, the wrong sign entirely). Same objective, same units, same model, same bits: one mechanism change was predicted by the proxy and the very next one was not. That is what an exhausted proxy looks like, and it is why "minimise the weighted objective harder" is not a plan.

**Attribution.** The same exact-rounding search run *without* W1 (plain L2 objective, `c12p4r`) improves KL 16% (1.121 to 0.940), against the 11% degradation it causes on top of W1. Same search, opposite sign, decided by which objective it minimises. Exact rounding is not intrinsically an overfit: the displacement is a property of the weighted metric.

**Conclusion.** `c12p4wr` is *not* an outlier. The weighted-rel_err → KL relation is not a function of weighted rel_err alone; it also depends on *how* the error was obtained. Two fits with the same weighted error, one reached by per-coefficient nearest rounding and one by exact search over the same grid, do measurably different amounts of behavioural damage, and the tighter one does more. The proxy has stopped being a proxy and become a coordinate.

## 4. Verdict: which proxy where, and what a better objective must do

| regime | trust | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing an outlier budget inside one fit mechanism | **either proxy** | §1b: Spearman +1.000 in every family measured |
| Comparing bit budgets (`c8p3` vs `c12p4`) | **plain rel_err** | §1a |
| Comparing fit *mechanisms* at identical bits | **neither; weighted is merely the only one with resolution** | §1c, §2 |
| Tightening the weighted objective further | **nothing we have** | §3: a strictly better objective value, worse KL, whole family displaced |

Stated so the next measurement can falsify them:

- **C1.** *Within a fixed fit mechanism, both proxies rank KL correctly.* Falsified by any family whose budget sweep has Spearman < 1. Six families, two models, no counterexample yet.
- **C2.** *Plain per-tensor `rel_err` cannot compare fit mechanisms.* The whole W1 effect fits inside a 0.52% move of it (elasticity 273 against 23.5 in its own family), and across the matched-bits groups it spans about 1% while KL spans 14.6x: there is no resolution there to rank anything with, whatever its rank statistic happens to come out at (§1c). Any objective proposal justified by plain rel_err alone should be rejected on this evidence.
- **C3.** *Lowering the current activation-weighted L2 further does not lower KL.* Measured once, at the frontier, by a mechanism (W2) that provably lowers the objective and changes nothing else. This is the claim the recalibration probe can still overturn: if `s` is badly estimated from 12 prompts, W2 was minimising a noisy objective more exactly, and a better-estimated `s` should restore alignment.
- **C4.** *A better objective must be mechanism-invariant.* The property the current one lacks is stated precisely in §3: fits reached by different mechanisms at equal objective value must land at equal KL. This is directly testable and needs no new objective; nearest vs exact rounding is already such a pair, and the current objective fails the test by 7-13% of KL. **Any candidate objective should be run through this test before it is run through a gate.**
- **C5.** *The objective's weights are a measurement whose error has never been quantified.* Both `s` (the fit weighting) and the awq outlier ranking are mean |activation| over **12 prompts**. At the time of this analysis no experiment had varied that number. Until it is varied, every statement about "the weighted objective" is a statement about the weighted objective *as estimated from 12 prompts*.

**What this analysis recommended.** Do not fund a new objective on this evidence. Two much cheaper measurements dominate it:

1. **The recalibration probe**: re-estimate `s` from 256 prompts and refit W1. Tests C5 directly, and its outcome decides whether C3 is a statement about the objective or about our estimate of it. This probe was subsequently run; the `@wt256`, `@wt128a`, and `@wt128b` rows above and the README's finding 4 are its result.
2. **One missing row**: `c12p4r`, W2 *without* W1, to say whether the §3 displacement is a property of exact rounding as such or only of exact rounding under the weighted metric. This row was subsequently run (it is in the table above) and it decided the question: see the attribution note in §3.

## 5. Honest limits

- **n is small and structured.** 34 seed rows in 10 families of four. Within a family the four rows are not independent draws: they are one fit plus three refits that *reuse* it, differing only in an outlier budget that is monotone in bits. A Spearman of +1 on such a family is worth far less than +1 on four independent samples, and its exact two-sided p cannot go below 0.083 however clean the ordering is.
- **The 4.17x W1 comparison in §2's table crosses two variables.** `c12p4_awq_p1.0` was fitted at 16,384 candidate seeds and `c12p4w_awq_p1.0` at 65,535. More seeds means a better fit under whichever objective is active. The one-variable control (`c12p4_awq_p1.0` at 65,535 seeds, KL 1.121121) decomposes it into a 1.62x seed-budget factor and a 2.57x objective factor: the W1 effect is 2.6x, and the two-variable pair is retained in §2 only as the historical comparison. For calibration of the seed-budget factor elsewhere: on `c8p3`/1.7B the same 16,384 → 65,535 step moved KL 1.552 → 0.845 (1.8x) under the plain objective.
- **The weighted proxy for the unweighted 1.7B rows was computed here, not on the original stack.** For `c8p3` the local cache holds the complete fit (196/196) and the number is exact; for `c12p4` it holds 80-81 of 196 tensors and the number is a subsample mean. The subsample's plain rel_err tracks the full row's to 3-4 decimal places (§0), which is the only representativeness evidence offered. A second, smaller caveat: those cached reconstructions were produced on a laptop GPU and the row's KL on a rented 4090. Fits are deterministic from `generator_seed` and the project relies on that to hand winners between machines, but cross-device fp agreement has only ever been checked to ~1e-7 (the W2 selftest note), not bit-exactly.
- **The three pod "stacks" are one machine image with three container ids.** Treated as distinct per the determinism rule, which costs power for no physical reason; the rows they share are bit-identical, which is itself evidence the rule is being conservative here.
- **One model carries the entire objective comparison.** Every weighted row in existence is Qwen3-1.7B. A 0.6B W1 twin was never produced (the machine that would have run it failed and the run was not repeated), so nothing here is known to transfer across scale, and the project's central lesson was precisely that per-tensor error is scale-invariant while damage is not.
- **KL is itself a proxy**: teacher-forced, 12 prompts, 692 positions, against a bf16 reference. The calibration-set question this document raises about `s` applies to it too, and no downstream-benchmark row (`--stage lmeval`) exists for any weighted variant.
- **No causal claim is made about *why* the §3 displacement exists.** Two mechanisms fit the data equally well: (i) `s` is noisy, so exact minimisation overfits the noise; (ii) weighted L2 genuinely diverges from KL near the optimum. Nothing measured here separates them; the recalibration probe does.
